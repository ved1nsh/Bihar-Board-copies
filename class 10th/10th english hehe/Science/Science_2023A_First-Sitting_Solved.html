Q.

6 marks
Describe the basic structure and working of a biogas plant.
Answer

Biogas plants are of two types —

(i) Fixed-dome type — In this, the dome-shaped gas tank always remains stationary.

(ii) Gas holder or floating-drum type — In this, the gas tank floats on water.

Construction of the floating gas-holder biogas plant: It consists of a well-shaped underground tank T, called the digester tank, which is made of bricks. A slurry of cow dung and water is filled into this tank. A drum-shaped steel gas tank H rests on top of the slurry; it has a gas outlet S fitted with a gas valve V. For supplying biogas to houses, the gas is drawn from outlet S.

Working: Equal quantities of cow dung and water are mixed to prepare a slurry. This is fed through the inlet pipe into the digester tank T and is kept there for about 50–60 days. During this period, anaerobic micro-organisms break down the slurry, producing biogas which collects in the tank H. The biogas is then carried to houses through pipes connected to the outlet S.

Find the pH value of a 10⁻³ M NaOH solution.
Answer

Solution:

pOH = −log[OH⁻] = −log[10⁻³] = −[(−3) log 10] = 3
∵ pH + pOH = 14 ⇒ pH = 14 − pOH = 14 − 3 = 11

Therefore, pH = 11 (Answer)

5 marks
Write the name and formula of one major ore of iron. How is this ore concentrated?
Answer

The main ore of iron is haematite (Fe₂O₃).

Reduction in the blast furnace: A mixture of calcined ore (8 parts), coke (4 parts) and limestone (1 part) is fed into the furnace. The temperature is higher at the bottom of the furnace and lower at the top. Ferric oxide is reduced to iron by CO —

CO₂ + C → 2CO↑ 3Fe₂O₃ + CO → 2Fe₃O₄ + CO₂↑ (≈300°C) Fe₃O₄ + CO → 3FeO + CO₂↑ (≈500°C) FeO + CO → Fe + CO₂↑ (≈700°C)

A blast of hot air is blown in at the lower part of the furnace through tuyeres. The temperature at the lower part is about 1600°C and at the upper end is about 400°C. In this way, spongy iron is obtained.

What is radiocarbon dating?
Answer

Radiocarbon dating is a method of determining the age of the remains of animals and plants. Carbon-14 is used for this purpose. This element is found in all living organisms. Carbon-14 is a radioactive isotope of carbon, with a half-life of about 5,730 years.

What is pollination? Define cross-pollination.
Answer

The process by which pollen grains are released from the anther and reach the stigma of the same flower or another flower of the same species is called pollination.

Cross-pollination: When pollen grains of one flower reach the stigma of a flower borne on another plant, the process is called cross-pollination.

Q.
26
2 marks
What is saprophytic nutrition? Answer with examples.
Answer

Saprophytic nutrition: In this type of nutrition, organisms absorb their food, in the form of dissolved organic substances, from the bodies of dead animals and plants through the surface of their own body. Such organisms are called saprophytes or saprotrophs.

Nutrition in many fungi, bacteria and some protozoa takes place by this method. They take in their food mainly in the liquid form through absorption.

Q.
28
2 marks
Nutrition is essential for living organisms. Why?
Answer

Every organism has two major requirements —

(i) a continuous supply of energy to carry out the metabolic activities of life, and

(ii) the formation of protoplasm for the growth of the body and for the repair of worn-out tissues.

To meet these requirements and to maintain the continuity of life, every organism needs food throughout its lifetime. Hence, nutrition is essential for living organisms.

Questions 29–30 are long-answer type. Answer any 1 of them. (1 × 5 = 5)
Q.
30
5 marks
What is tissue culture? How is it carried out?
Answer

Tissue culture is the process by which various body tissues or cells can be grown in an external medium under suitable conditions.

Procedure: A small piece of tissue is cut from a healthy, desired plant. It is placed in a vessel containing a nutrient medium. Under suitable temperature, humidity and favourable conditions, this piece of tissue grows into an unorganised mass called a callus. A small portion of the callus is then transferred to a hormone-containing medium, where it develops and differentiates to form a plantlet. These plantlets are transplanted into pots or into the ground, where they grow into adult plants. The plants produced by this method are called clones. New plants of chrysanthemum, asparagus, orchids, etc., are produced by this method.
