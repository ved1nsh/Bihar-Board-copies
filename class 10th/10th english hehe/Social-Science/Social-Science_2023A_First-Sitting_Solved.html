What was the main objective of the government formed under Kerensky's leadership after the March Revolution?
Ans.

After the March Revolution, a government of liberal socialists was formed under Kerensky's leadership. Its main objectives were — to establish a democratic and constitutional government, to continue the war with the help of the Allied powers, to protect private property, to solve the land problem through the Constituent Assembly, and to bring changes in institutions through legal means. However, the Bolsheviks did not accept this government.

Why did Lenin announce the New Economic Policy?
Ans.

Lenin was a skilful social thinker and a practical politician. He realised that it was not possible to put a complete socialist system in place at once, or to confront the entire capitalist world all at the same time. Therefore, in 1921 he announced the New Economic Policy, in which he had to compromise to some extent with Marxist principles. The aim of this policy was to learn from past experience and take practical steps.

What do you understand by the Rowlatt Act?
Ans.

Lord Chelmsford appointed a committee under the chairmanship of Justice Sidney Rowlatt, and on its recommendation the Rowlatt Act was passed on 25 March, 1919. Under this Act, any person could be arrested on inadmissible evidence and without a warrant, and no appeal could be made against such arrest. Despite strong opposition from Indian leaders, the bill was enforced.

How did English education play an important role in the rise of Indian nationalism?
Ans.

The British spread English education in India out of their own self-interest, yet the Western thought of the 19th century and English education ended the mental stagnation of Indians and gave them an opportunity to adopt a modern, rational and reasoned political outlook. Indians now began to understand the importance of principles such as liberty, equality and representation.

English education helped Indians to understand the political significance of the American War of Independence, the French Revolution and the revolutions in Italy, Spain and Greece. The educated class was influenced by the ideas of thinkers like Rousseau, Mazzini and Garibaldi. A common education system also helped in the unification of the country. As a result, Indians began to regard foreign rule as humiliating and started organising themselves to secure their rights.

Write the sources of income of a Municipal Corporation.
Ans.

The main sources of income of a Municipal Corporation are as follows—

(i) House Tax — A fixed percentage of the taxable value of a property is collected from house owners.

(ii) Water Tax — A fixed percentage (about 3%) of the taxable value of houses and shops is collected as water tax.

(iii) Entertainment Tax — A tax levied on cinema halls, theatres, circuses, etc. In addition, tax is also collected on advertisement banners, hoardings, signboards and so on.

How does a federal system help to strengthen the values of national unity?
Ans.

A federal system is generally formed in two ways —

(i) Several independent and sovereign states come together and accept a common sovereignty to form a federal state; for example, the United States of America, Switzerland and Australia. In such a system the autonomy of the states is stronger and the residuary powers remain with the states.

(ii) A federation is also formed by dividing powers between the provincial and central governments; for example, India, Belgium and Spain. Here the central government is more powerful and the residuary powers are given to the centre.

A federal system works with a twin purpose — respecting regional and other diversities, and safeguarding and promoting the unity of the country. Whenever needed, special rights may also be granted to a particular state (as in the case of Jammu & Kashmir, Sikkim, etc., in India). In this way federalism maintains unity amidst diversity and promotes national unity.

Establish the relationship between income and poverty.
Ans.

Income and poverty are directly related. A person or family whose income is so low that it cannot meet its minimum needs — food, clothing, housing, education and health — is called poor. When income rises, people are able to meet their needs and poverty decreases; when income falls, poverty increases. Therefore, income is a major basis for measuring poverty.

Describe the components of globalisation in detail.
Ans.

The five main components of globalisation are as follows—

(i) Reduction of trade barriers — Trade barriers are reduced so that goods and services can be exchanged freely. Foreign companies can sell their products in the Indian market and Indian companies can sell theirs abroad.

(ii) Free flow of capital — An environment in which capital can flow freely between different countries. Foreign investors can invest in India and Indians can invest abroad.

(iii) Free flow of technology — Technology can be imported and exported from any country without restrictions.

(iv) Free flow of labour — Labour can move freely between different countries.

(v) Full convertibility of capital — This is also an essential element of globalisation.

Explain the concept of sustainable development.
Ans.

Resources are gifts of nature. Out of selfishness, human beings have indiscriminately exploited natural resources without thinking, which has given rise to many environmental problems such as global warming, depletion of the ozone layer, soil erosion and acid rain.

The idea that resources should be used in a planned manner, that no harm should be done to the environment, and that present development should be carried on while keeping in mind the needs of future generations — this idea is called sustainable development. It can keep both the present and the future safe.

Suggest measures to reduce the destructive effects of a tsunami.
Ans.

To reduce the severity of a tsunami, the following measures should be taken—

(i) Early warning is essential to guard against a tsunami.

(ii) Stations and platforms need to be set up out at sea, which can study the horizontal movements of sea water and send signals to the coast, so that people can be evacuated in time.

(iii) Vegetation such as mangroves should be planted along the coastline.

(iv) Concrete embankments should be built, which will help block the waves striking the shore.
