What is carbylamine reaction ?
Ans

When a primary amine is heated with chloroform (CHCl₃) and alcoholic potassium hydroxide (KOH), it forms an isocyanide (carbylamine) which has a very bad, offensive smell. This reaction is called the carbylamine reaction. It is used as a test for primary amines.

R–NH₂ + CHCl₃ + 3KOH → R–NC + 3KCl + 3H₂O

Why do transition elements form complex compounds ?
Ans

Transition metals form complex (coordination) compounds because they have small size, high effective nuclear charge, and empty valence d-orbitals that can accept lone pairs of electrons from ligands (Lewis bases). Since the ligands have at least one lone pair of electrons that can be donated to the metal ion, transition metals readily form coordinate bonds and hence complex compounds.

2 marks
Explain effective atomic number.
Ans

The Effective Atomic Number (EAN) of a metal in a complex is the total number of electrons present on the central metal atom after it has accepted electron pairs from the ligands. It is calculated as:

EAN = (Atomic number of metal) − (Number of electrons lost in forming the ion) + (2 × Number of ligands donating electron pairs)

If the EAN equals the atomic number of the nearest noble gas, the complex is highly stable. For example, in [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻, EAN of Fe = 26 − 2 + (2 × 6) = 36, which equals the atomic number of krypton.

What is mole fraction ?
Ans

Mole fraction is the ratio of the number of moles of one component (solute or solvent) to the total number of moles of all the components present in the solution.

Mole fraction = (Number of moles of the component) / (Total number of moles of all components)

For a binary mixture of A and B with moles n_A and n_B :

x_A = n_A / (n_A + n_B) ; x_B = n_B / (n_A + n_B)

Mole fraction is a ratio, so it has no unit.

Q
10
2 marks
Write Raoult's law of relative lowering of vapour pressure.
Ans

Raoult's law: For a solution containing a non-volatile solute, the relative lowering of vapour pressure of the solvent is equal to the mole fraction of the solute in the solution.

(P° − P) / P° = x_solute

where P° = vapour pressure of pure solvent, P = vapour pressure of solution, and x_solute = mole fraction of the solute.

What do you understand by rate of a reaction ? What factors affect the rate of a reaction ? Discuss.
Ans

Rate of a reaction: The rate of a chemical reaction is the change in concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.

Rate = − (decrease in concentration of reactant) / time = + (increase in concentration of product) / time

Factors affecting the rate of a reaction:

(i) Concentration of reactants: Increasing the concentration of reactants generally increases the rate, as more molecules collide per unit time.

(ii) Temperature: Increasing temperature increases the rate, because molecules gain more kinetic energy and collide more often and more effectively.

(iii) Nature of reactants: Reactions between ions are usually fast, while those between covalent molecules are slower.

(iv) Catalyst: A catalyst increases the rate of a reaction by providing an alternative path with lower activation energy.

(v) Surface area: A larger surface area of solid reactants (e.g. powder) increases the rate.

(vi) Presence of light: Some reactions (photochemical reactions) become faster in the presence of light.

5 marks
What is soap ? How does it act in the cleansing of clothes ?
Ans

Soap: Soaps are sodium or potassium salts of long-chain fatty acids (such as stearic acid, palmitic acid). They are made by heating fats or oils with sodium hydroxide; this process is called saponification.

Fat/Oil + NaOH → Soap + Glycerol

Cleansing action of soap: A soap molecule has two parts — a long hydrocarbon tail which is hydrophobic (water-repelling, oil-loving) and a charged head (–COO⁻Na⁺) which is hydrophilic (water-loving).

When soap is added to dirty water, the hydrophobic tails of soap molecules attach to oil/grease and dirt particles, while the hydrophilic heads remain in water. The soap molecules surround each dirt particle to form a tiny cluster called a micelle.

In a micelle, the dirt is trapped inside and the outer water-loving heads keep it suspended in water. On rinsing, these micelles are washed away along with the dirt, leaving the cloth clean.

Write the principle of manufacture of ammonia by Haber's process. How does it react with CuSO₄ solution ?
Ans

Haber's process: Ammonia is manufactured by the direct combination of nitrogen and hydrogen in the ratio 1 : 3 by volume.

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) + heat

Principle (favourable conditions): Since the reaction is reversible, exothermic, and proceeds with a decrease in volume, according to Le Chatelier's principle the maximum yield of ammonia is obtained at:

(i) High pressure (about 200 atm).

(ii) Low temperature (about 450–500 °C is used as an optimum, since too low a temperature makes the reaction very slow).

(iii) A catalyst of finely divided iron with molybdenum as a promoter.

Reaction with CuSO₄ solution: When ammonia is passed into copper sulphate solution, it first forms a pale blue precipitate of copper hydroxide, which dissolves in excess ammonia to give a deep blue solution of the tetraamminecopper(II) complex:

CuSO₄ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄]SO₄ (deep blue)
