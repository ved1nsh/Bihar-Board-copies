What is incomplete dominance ?
Ans

Incomplete dominance is a type of inheritance in which neither of the two alleles of a gene is completely dominant over the other. As a result, the heterozygous offspring (F₁) shows a mixed or intermediate character between the two parents.

For example, when a true-breeding red-flowered plant is crossed with a true-breeding white-flowered plant (e.g. in Antirrhinum / snapdragon), the F₁ plants have pink flowers — an intermediate of red and white.

What would be the blood group of offspring, if the blood group of mother is AB and blood group of father is O ?
Ans

If the blood group of the mother is AB (genotype I^A I^B) and the blood group of the father is O (genotype ii), then the offspring will receive one allele from each parent.

Possible offspring genotypes are I^A i and I^B i.

Therefore, the blood group of the offspring will be A or B.

What are the goals of Human Genome Project ?
Ans

Goals of the Human Genome Project :

(i) To develop ways of mapping the human genome at increasingly fine levels of precision.

(ii) To identify all the genes in human DNA.

(iii) To determine the sequence of the 3 billion chemical base pairs that make up human DNA.

(iv) To store this information in databases and develop tools for data analysis.

(v) To address the ethical, legal and social issues that may arise from the project.

(vi) To transfer related technologies to other sectors such as industries.

What is the relationship between thorns of Bougainvillea and tendrils of Cucurbita ?
Ans

The tendrils of Cucurbita and the thorns of Bougainvillea are homologous organs, because they are similar in origin — both are modified branches and axial in position. However, they perform different functions: in Bougainvillea the thorns are modified for protection, while in Cucurbita the tendrils are used for climbing. This shows divergent evolution.

Explain parasitic adaptations.
Ans

Parasitic adaptation refers to the evolutionary traits and behaviours that parasites develop to survive within their host organism. These adaptations can include specialised structures for attachment, evasion of the host's immune responses, and mechanisms for obtaining nutrients from the host. Essentially, parasites have evolved to exploit their hosts for survival and reproduction.

What is cell mediated immunity ?
Ans

Cell-mediated immunity is a branch of the immune system that involves the activation of immune cells, particularly T lymphocytes (T cells), to defend the body against intracellular pathogens such as some bacteria and parasites, as well as abnormal cells like cancer cells.

Ans

Cyclosporin-A is a medication that belongs to a class of drugs called immunosuppressants. Explain the causes of biodiversity loss.
Ans

Biodiversity loss means the decline or disappearance of the variety of living organisms from an area. The main causes are :

(i) Habitat loss and fragmentation: Cutting of forests, draining of wetlands and conversion of natural land for agriculture, housing and industry destroys the homes of many species. This is the most important cause of biodiversity loss.

(ii) Over-exploitation: Excessive hunting, fishing and collection of plants and animals for food, medicine and trade reduces their numbers, sometimes to extinction.

(iii) Alien (exotic) species invasion: When new species are introduced into an area, they may compete with, prey upon or bring disease to native species, causing their decline. For example, the introduction of the Nile perch led to the extinction of many Cichlid fish in Lake Victoria.

(iv) Co-extinction: When one species becomes extinct, the other species that depend on it (such as its parasites or partners) also become extinct.

(v) Pollution and climate change: Air, water and soil pollution and global warming disturb natural habitats and harm sensitive species.

Write short notes on the following : (A) Phosphorus cycle (B) Primary immunity
Ans

(A) Phosphorus Cycle

Phosphorus is a major constituent of biological membranes, nucleic acids, cellular energy transfer systems (ATP), and also of shells, bones and teeth. The natural reservoir of phosphorus is rock, which contains phosphorus in the form of phosphates.

When rocks are weathered, minute amounts of these phosphates dissolve in solution and are absorbed by the roots of plants. Herbivores and other animals obtain this element from plants. The waste products and dead organisms are decomposed by phosphate-solubilising bacteria, releasing phosphorus back into the soil.

(B) Primary Immunity

Primary immunity refers to the initial immune response mounted by the body upon encountering a specific pathogen or antigen for the first time. This response involves the activation and proliferation of immune cells, such as T cells and B cells, and the production of antibodies tailored to target the specific pathogen.

Write short notes on the following : (A) Allergy (B) Chemical composition of DNA
Ans

(A) Allergy

Allergy is an exaggerated (hypersensitive) response of the immune system to certain harmless substances in the environment, called allergens (such as pollen, dust, mites, or certain foods). When the body is exposed to an allergen, it produces a large amount of IgE antibodies, which cause the release of chemicals like histamine and serotonin. These chemicals produce symptoms such as sneezing, watery eyes, running nose, difficulty in breathing, and skin rashes. Allergy can be treated with drugs like antihistamines and steroids.

(B) Chemical composition of DNA

DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is composed of nucleotides, which consist of three main components — a sugar molecule (deoxyribose), a phosphate group, and one of four nitrogenous bases: adenine (A), thymine (T), cytosine (C) or guanine (G).

These nucleotides are arranged in a double-helix structure, with the sugar-phosphate backbone forming the two sides and the nitrogenous bases pairing in the centre through hydrogen bonds (A pairs with T, and C pairs with G).
