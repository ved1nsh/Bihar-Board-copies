What is metamerism?
Ans

Metamerism is a type of structural isomerism in organic chemistry in which compounds with the same molecular formula have different alkyl groups attached to a polyvalent functional group (such as –O–, –S–, –NH–, –CO–). For example, diethyl ether ($\mathrm{C_2H_5-O-C_2H_5}$) and methyl propyl ether ($\mathrm{CH_3-O-C_3H_7}$) are metamers.

What are chiral and achiral compounds?
Ans

Chiral compounds are molecules that are non-superimposable on their mirror images, much like a pair of hands. The presence of a chiral centre — a carbon atom bonded to four different groups — is a common cause of chirality.

Achiral compounds, on the other hand, are superimposable on their mirror images; they are identical to their mirror image, like a coffee cup.

Essential amino acids are the nine protein-building blocks that the body cannot synthesize on its own and must be obtained through the diet. "$\mathrm{O_3}$ behaves as a strong oxidising agent." Explain.
Ans

Ozone ($\mathrm{O_3}$) is a powerful oxidising agent because it readily decomposes to form diatomic oxygen and a highly reactive "nascent" oxygen atom [O]:

$$\mathrm{O_3 \rightarrow O_2 + [O]}$$

What is slag?
Ans

Slag is an impure, fusible material formed during the extraction of metals. It is formed by the chemical reaction between the flux added to remove impurities from the ore and the infusible impurities (gangue) present in the ore. It is lighter than the molten metal and floats on top of it, from where it is later removed by decantation. For example, during the extraction of iron:

$$\mathrm{CaO + SiO_2 \rightarrow CaSiO_3 \,(slag)}$$

Write Faraday's 2nd law of Electrolysis.
Ans

Faraday's second law of electrolysis states that when the same quantity of electricity is passed through different electrolytes, the masses of the various substances deposited or liberated at the electrodes are directly proportional to their chemical equivalent weights. Mathematically, if the same charge $Q$ is passed:

$$\frac{W_1}{W_2} = \frac{E_1}{E_2}$$

where $W$ is the mass deposited and $E$ is the equivalent weight of the substance.

$$k = A\,e^{-E_a/RT}$$

Why is $\mathrm{PCl_5}$ a known compound, but $\mathrm{NCl_5}$ is not?
Ans

Nitrogen in $\mathrm{NCl_3}$ cannot form $\mathrm{NCl_5}$ because it belongs to the 2nd period and has no vacant d orbitals, so it cannot expand its octet beyond 8 electrons.

Phosphorus in $\mathrm{PCl_5}$ belongs to the 3rd period, has vacant 3d orbitals, and can therefore expand its octet to form five bonds — hence $\mathrm{PCl_5}$ exists but $\mathrm{NCl_5}$ does not.

Why are alcohols more soluble in water than alkanes of same molecular weight?
Ans

Alcohols are more soluble in water than alkanes of the same molecular weight because the hydroxyl (–OH) group in alcohols can form hydrogen bonds with water molecules. What do you understand by elevation of boiling point?
Ans

Elevation of boiling point is the phenomenon in which a pure solvent's boiling point increases after a non-volatile solute is added to it, forming a solution. This increase is a colligative property, meaning it depends on the number of solute particles in the solvent, not their type. For example, when salt is added to water, the boiling point of the saltwater solution is higher than that of pure water. Mathematically, $\Delta T_b = K_b \cdot m$, where $K_b$ is the molal boiling point elevation constant and $m$ is molality.

Ans

The main difference is that alcohols have a hydroxyl (–OH) group bonded to an aliphatic (carbon chain) carbon, while phenols have an –OH group bonded directly to an aromatic (benzene) ring. Explain Raoult's law. How would you distinguish between ideal and non-ideal solutions on its basis?
Ans

Raoult's Law: Raoult's law states that, for a solution of volatile components, the partial vapour pressure of a component in the solution is directly proportional to its mole fraction in the solution. Mathematically:

$$P_A = X_A \cdot P_A^0$$

where $P_A$ is the partial vapour pressure of component A in the solution, $X_A$ is its mole fraction, and $P_A^0$ is the vapour pressure of pure A. For a binary mixture of A and B, the total vapour pressure $P_T = X_A P_A^0 + X_B P_B^0$.

This law applies to solutions where intermolecular interactions between A–A, B–B and A–B molecules are similar.

Distinguishing Ideal and Non-Ideal Solutions:

(i) An ideal solution perfectly obeys Raoult's law at all compositions and temperatures. In such solutions, the total vapour pressure varies linearly with composition, indicating that interactions between unlike (A–B) molecules are essentially the same as those between like (A–A or B–B) molecules. Also, $\Delta H_{mix} = 0$ and $\Delta V_{mix} = 0$. Example: benzene + toluene.

(ii) A non-ideal solution shows deviations from Raoult's law. These deviations can be positive or negative depending on whether the interactions between unlike molecules are weaker or stronger than those between like molecules.

• Positive deviation: When intermolecular forces between A and B are weaker than those between like molecules, the vapour pressure is higher than predicted ($\Delta H_{mix} > 0$). Example: ethanol + acetone.

• Negative deviation: When A–B interactions are stronger than A–A and B–B interactions, the vapour pressure is lower than predicted ($\Delta H_{mix} < 0$). Example: chloroform + acetone.

What do you understand by concentration of an ore? Discuss the different methods of concentration of ores.
Ans

Concentration of an Ore: The concentration of an ore (also called dressing or beneficiation) refers to the process of removing impurities (gangue) from the ore to increase the content of the desired metal or mineral. Ores are usually found mixed with unwanted materials like soil, sand or other minerals; concentration improves the metal content, making subsequent extraction easier and more economical.

Methods of Concentration of Ores:

1. Gravity Separation (Washing / Levigation):

• Works on the principle of difference in densities between the ore and the gangue.

• Heavy ore particles settle down, while lighter impurities are washed away using a stream of water.

• Example: Concentration of tin ore (cassiterite) and gold ore (placer deposits).

2. Magnetic Separation:

• Used when either the ore or the gangue is magnetic.

• The crushed ore is passed over a magnetic separator (a belt running over magnetic rollers); magnetic particles are attracted and separated from non-magnetic ones.

• Example: Separation of magnetite ($\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$) and chromite from gangue.

3. Froth Flotation:

• Used mainly for sulphide ores.

• The powdered ore is mixed with water, oil (e.g. pine oil) and a frothing agent. Air is blown through the mixture.

• The ore particles attach to the oil-coated air bubbles and float to the top as a froth that is skimmed off, while the gangue sinks.

• Example: Lead, zinc and copper sulphide ores (galena, zinc blende, copper pyrites).

4. Leaching:

• Some ores are soluble in suitable solvents.

• The ore is treated with a solvent that selectively dissolves the metal compound, leaving the gangue behind. The metal is then recovered from the solution.

• Example: Extraction of aluminium from bauxite using NaOH (Bayer's process); extraction of silver and gold using NaCN.

Methanoic acid has the chemical formula HCOOH and contains only one carbon atom. It is the simplest carboxylic acid and is mainly used as a disinfectant and in the chemical industry. It is also called formic acid and is found naturally in ant bites.

It is also called acetic acid, is used to add flavour to food (vinegar is its dilute aqueous solution), and is widely used in the manufacture of plastics like cellulose acetate.

In summary, methanoic acid contains only one carbon atom, whereas ethanoic acid contains two carbons and has a more complex structure with an alkyl substituent.

What are carbohydrates? How are they classified?
Ans

What are Carbohydrates:

Carbohydrates are organic compounds composed mainly of carbon (C), hydrogen (H) and oxygen (O), generally following the formula $(CH_2O)_n$. They are commonly known as sugars or starches and serve as a primary source of energy for the body. Chemically, they are polyhydroxy aldehydes or polyhydroxy ketones, or compounds that give such on hydrolysis.

Classification of Carbohydrates:

1. Monosaccharides:

• These are the simplest carbohydrates and cannot be hydrolyzed into simpler sugars.

• General formula: $(CH_2O)_n$, where $n = 3$–$7$.

• Examples: Glucose, Fructose, Galactose, Ribose.

2. Disaccharides:

• Formed by the joining of two monosaccharide units through a glycosidic bond.

• Can be hydrolyzed to give two monosaccharides.

• Examples: Sucrose (glucose + fructose), Lactose (glucose + galactose), Maltose (glucose + glucose).

3. Polysaccharides:

• Formed by the polymerization of many monosaccharide units.

• Can be soluble or insoluble in water.

• Examples: Starch, Glycogen, Cellulose, Chitin.

On the basis of reducing nature, carbohydrates may also be classified as reducing (e.g. glucose, maltose) and non-reducing (e.g. sucrose) sugars.

(a) Differentiate between DNA and RNA. (b) What are nucleic acids?
Ans

(a) Difference between DNA and RNA:

DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) and RNA (Ribonucleic Acid) are both nucleic acids, but they differ in several ways:

• Sugar: DNA contains deoxyribose, while RNA contains ribose.

• Structure: DNA is usually double-stranded (double helix), whereas RNA is single-stranded.

• Nitrogenous Bases: DNA has adenine (A), guanine (G), cytosine (C) and thymine (T); RNA has uracil (U) instead of thymine.

• Location: DNA is mainly found in the nucleus, while RNA is found in both the nucleus and cytoplasm.

• Function: DNA stores genetic information, whereas RNA plays a role in protein synthesis.

(b) Nucleic Acids:

Nucleic acids are biological macromolecules found in DNA and RNA. They are composed of carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen and phosphorus. Their structural units are nucleotides — each nucleotide consists of a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar and a phosphate group. Nucleic acids are responsible for storing and transmitting genetic information and play a crucial role in protein synthesis in living cells.

Differentiate between Electrochemical cell and Electrolytic cell. What are the importances of salt bridge in an electrochemical cell?
Ans

Difference between Electrochemical Cell and Electrolytic Cell:

1. Definition:

• Electrochemical (Galvanic) Cell: A device that converts chemical energy into electrical energy spontaneously.

• Electrolytic Cell: A device that uses electrical energy to drive a non-spontaneous chemical reaction.

2. Energy Flow:

• Electrochemical cell → Chemical energy → Electrical energy.

• Electrolytic cell → Electrical energy → Chemical energy.

3. Spontaneity:

• Electrochemical cell → Reaction is spontaneous ($\Delta G < 0$).

• Electrolytic cell → Reaction is non-spontaneous ($\Delta G > 0$).

4. Electrodes:

• Electrochemical cell → Anode is negative, cathode is positive.

• Electrolytic cell → Anode is positive, cathode is negative.

5. Example:

• Electrochemical cell → Daniell cell (Zn/Cu).

• Electrolytic cell → Electrolysis of molten NaCl.

Importance of Salt Bridge in an Electrochemical Cell:

1. Maintains electrical neutrality in the two half-cells by allowing the flow of ions between the two electrolytes.

2. Prevents mixing of the two different electrolytic solutions that could otherwise react directly and short-circuit the cell.

3. Completes the electrical circuit, allowing the flow of electrons through the external wire and ions through the internal solution; without the salt bridge, no current would flow.
