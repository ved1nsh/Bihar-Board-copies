Write an essay on any ONE of the following topics in about 150-200 words:
(a) Moral Values   (b) Role of Education   (c) Advantages and Disadvantages of Mobile Phones   (d) India of My Dreams   (e) My School Life
Ans

(c) Advantages and Disadvantages of Mobile Phones — Full model essay

The mobile phone is one of the greatest gifts of modern science. It has completely changed the way we live, learn and communicate. From a simple call to attending an online class, from sending an email to making digital payments, almost everything is now possible in the palm of our hand.

Advantages: Mobile phones make communication instant. Distance no longer matters; a student in a village can speak face to face with a teacher in a city. They offer access to the entire internet, which is a huge library of knowledge. We can attend online lectures, watch educational videos, use Google Maps, book tickets, do net-banking, take photographs and listen to music. In emergencies, a single call can save a life.

Disadvantages: Excessive use leads to addiction, weak eyesight, headaches, and poor concentration in studies. Many students waste hours on social media and games. Cyber-crime, online frauds and the leakage of personal information have become serious threats. Mobile phones also reduce face-to-face interaction within families.

Conclusion: A mobile phone is neither good nor bad in itself; it depends on the user. If we use it wisely, it becomes a powerful tool for progress; if misused, it can destroy our time, health and character.

Outlines for the other topics:

(a) Moral Values — Principles guiding right conduct; honesty, kindness, respect, responsibility, truthfulness, compassion; taught first at home, reinforced in school and by society; foundation of a fair, harmonious society; corruption, violence and selfishness thrive when moral values are absent; without moral values, even the most educated person is dangerous.

(b) Role of Education — Empowers individuals with knowledge, skills, attitudes and values; promotes critical thinking, problem-solving, communication; instills discipline and social awareness; reduces poverty; opens employment; broadens perspectives; not limited to academics — includes moral, physical and cultural development; the only permanent solution to poverty, ignorance and superstition.

(e) My School Life — The most memorable phase of life; learning, fun, friendships; teachers who shape character; co-curricular activities — debates, sports, scouts; lessons in discipline, teamwork, leadership and confidence; ups and downs of exams; the day I won a prize; a treasure-house of memories that will last forever.

Q
102
4 marks
Explain any ONE of the following extracts with reference to the context (1 × 4):
Ans

(a) "And as we walk, we make the pledge that we shall always march ahead."

Reference & Context: These lines are taken from Martin Luther King, Jr.'s famous speech "I Have a Dream". MLK is addressing the supporters of the Civil Rights movement at the Lincoln Memorial in Washington (1963).

Explanation: The "walk" here symbolises the non-violent march of the Black community towards racial equality. MLK exhorts his listeners to take a solemn pledge — that no matter how slow or difficult the journey is, they will never turn back. The phrase "march ahead" expresses unbreakable determination and faith in the future. It captures the spirit of the entire speech: courage, hope, and continued struggle for justice.

(b) "At one time, there were some quarrels about the Ox Meadows, you're quite right. But now, everyone knows they're mine."

Reference & Context: This dialogue is from Anton Chekhov's one-act farce "A Marriage Proposal". The line is spoken by Lomov in the house of Choobookov, when he has come to propose marriage to Natalia.

(a) Summary of any one poem:

(i) Macavity: The Mystery Cat — T. S. Eliot
"Macavity: The Mystery Cat" is T. S. Eliot's playful mock-detective poem from "Old Possum's Book of Practical Cats". D. H. Lawrence's "Snake" describes a hot Sicilian noon when the poet comes to fill his pitcher and finds a golden-brown snake at the trough. The snake drinks calmly, like a "king in exile". The snake disappears in undignified haste. Immediately the poet is filled with shame and self-loathing. He realises he has missed his chance with "one of the lords of life". Walter de la Mare's "An Epitaph" is a short, gentle lyric written in memory of a beautiful person of the West Country who is now dead. The poem then reflects on the bitter truth of mortality: however lovely a person may be, beauty, fame and even memory all fade with time. (i) Short Note on Old English: Old English, also called Anglo-Saxon, is the earliest historical form of the English language. It was spoken in England roughly from the 5th to the 11th century AD. It was brought to Britain by the Germanic tribes — Angles, Saxons and Jutes — after the Romans left. Old English was a heavily inflected language with four cases, three genders and strong/weak verbs, much closer to modern German than to modern English. Its vocabulary was almost entirely Germanic. The greatest surviving work of Old English is the epic poem "Beowulf". After the Norman Conquest of 1066, French heavily influenced Old English and gradually transformed it into Middle English.

(ii) Difference between a Short Story and a Novel: A short story is a brief work of prose fiction that can usually be read in one sitting; a novel is a long, sustained narrative covering many chapters. A short story has a single plot, a small number of characters and one dominant idea or "single effect" (Edgar Allan Poe); a novel has a complex plot with subplots, a large set of characters, multiple themes, and detailed settings. The short story aims at compression, intensity and a sudden climax; the novel aims at scope, depth and gradual development. Examples: Shiga Naoya's "The Artist" is a short story; Charles Dickens's "Oliver Twist" is a novel.

(iii) Difficulties of a Second-Language Learner: A second-language learner faces several difficulties. Pronunciation is hard because the sounds of L2 do not exist in L1 — for example, the English /th/ sound is difficult for Indians. Vocabulary is huge and many words have multiple meanings, idioms and shades of formality. Grammar of the second language often clashes with the mother-tongue pattern, leading to errors in tense, prepositions and article use. Spelling, with its silent letters, is unpredictable. Lack of an English-speaking environment outside the classroom slows learning. Cultural references in literature can confuse the learner. Constant practice, reading and patient correction are needed to overcome these difficulties.
